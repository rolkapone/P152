Exploration / not the default / Sprint 2

Liquid glass on the toolbar and the view bar

A material that behaves like thick liquid glass: edge refraction (SVG feDisplacementMap used as a backdrop-filter), specular highlights that follow the pointer, luminous rims, controls that stretch and morph with springs. Shown over the toolbar and the floating view bar, above a placeholder studio scene. Everything is local (no network): the displacement map is drawn in a canvas and injected as an inline data URI.

Conclusion

  • Not by default. Keep the v0.4 materials (variant A). Offer liquid glass later as an opt-in appearance (Help / Settings, with US-025), limited to the toolbar and the view bar.
  • Only the AA-safe variants (B, C) are shippable: they keep an 80 % tint under text and put the "liquid" in the rim (refraction, specular, sheen). The fully clear look (D) cannot meet 4.5:1 on a worst-case background (see the live table), so it would only be acceptable for glyph-only controls, never for labels.
  • Refraction only exists in Chromium (Chrome, Edge, Opera): Safari and Firefox ignore SVG filters in backdrop-filter. On those the same component falls back to variant B (still specular and premium). So the effect cannot be the identity of the product on Apple devices, where Safari is the browser.
  • Cost: each lens re-filters the backdrop every frame while the WebGL scene moves. Budget: at most 100 000 px of lens area (the toolbar + view bar here are about 90 000 px), automatic fallback to B if the frame time exceeds 20 ms. Use the FPS probe on this page with "Animate scene" to compare variants on your machine; I could not measure GPU cost in my environment.

1. Demo

Variant C is selected. Switch variant, theme and background (the black and white stripes are the worst cases of the contrast rule). Move the pointer: the highlight follows. Click the glass segmented control (the thumb stretches then settles with a spring) and the "Views" pill (it grows into a menu). These two morphing demos are CSS-only: a displacement map has a fixed size, so animating a lens through a size change would mean regenerating the map every frame (or a 9-slice map).

3. Cost, compatibility, fallback

Performance (GPU, next to the WebGL scene)

  • Every backdrop-filter region is re-rendered each time the content behind it changes. With a WebGL canvas that animates, that is every frame, for blur and again for the SVG filter.
  • The lens filter (feImage + feDisplacementMap) costs more than a plain blur, and is far more expensive on integrated and mobile GPUs. It scales with the lens area, not with the scene.
  • Guard rails: area budget (toolbar + view bar only), lens off while the user orbits the scene (static during drags, restored on idle), auto-degrade to variant B when frame time exceeds 20 ms, off when "Reduce transparency" or battery saver is on.
  • The performance budget of the product stays: at least 60 FPS desktop, 30 FPS recent phone. QA must measure A, B, C on the target machines before any decision.

Accessibility

  • AA-safe only with an 80 % tint under text (B, C). Clearer glass needs an opaque chip under every label (the verdict chip already is) or glyph-only controls.
  • Refraction distorts the content behind the rim only, never the text of the control itself.
  • Reduced motion: no stretch, no morph (instant states); reduced transparency: opaque --material-solid, no rim, no lens (shown by the switches above).
  • Focus ring and halo are unchanged and stay at 3:1 or more (see the table).

Browser compatibility

  • backdrop-filter blur and saturate: Chrome, Edge, Safari (with the -webkit- prefix), Firefox 103+.
  • backdrop-filter: url(#svg): Chromium only. Elsewhere the declaration must not be applied (here the lens is added by script only when Chromium is detected, otherwise B).
  • Masked gradient rim (mask-composite), linear() springs, container queries: all modern browsers.

If it is adopted later

  • New tokens (not added now): --lens-scale, --lens-bezel, --specular-*, a "Liquid" appearance preset beside the system theme.
  • Component LensGlass (generates and caches the map per size, observes resize, detects support, honours the three accessibility options).
  • Same component API as .material-regular: switching is a class, not a rewrite.
